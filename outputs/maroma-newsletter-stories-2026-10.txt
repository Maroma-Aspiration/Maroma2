HEADLINE
Incense at Maroma is still made by hand
================================================================================

Lead
Maroma still makes incense by hand in Auroville, using woods, resins, herbs, and flowers in a method the company traces to older Indian practice, when those same materials were burned to cleanse a room, support prayer, and quiet the mind.

Intro
Maroma says it keeps that sequence on the bench: select, blend, shape, and dry each stick.

Follow-up
The company said the ingredients, the people who form them, and the time the process takes remain central to the work. Smoke, in this account, is treated as part of a room ritual, not only as a scent.

The incense range is sold as charcoal-free and made without phthalates. It is listed on maroma.com.

HEADLINE
World Environmental Health Day, 26 September
================================================================================

Lead
On World Environmental Health Day, 26 September, Maroma set out how it runs its Auroville workshop, pointing to solar power, less plastic in packaging, and the sorting of materials for recycling as work already in place.

Intro
The company presented these steps as current practice, not a later pledge.

Follow-up
Split and leftover candle wax is reused. Some is poured into special candles. Some is sold at a lower price to the Auroville community.

Maroma described the aim as reducing waste, reusing what it can, and making careful choices at each stage of production.
