The Art of Incense, Made by Hand

At Maroma, incense making remains a hands-on craft, rooted in India's long tradition of working with natural aromatic materials.

In our Auroville workshop, every incense stick begins with a carefully considered blend of woods, resins, herbs and flowers. These ingredients have been part of Indian rituals for generations, traditionally burned to purify spaces, accompany prayer and encourage moments of stillness.

Today, our artisans carry this practice forward through a process of selecting, blending, shaping and drying each stick by hand. It is work that calls for patience, experience and a sensitivity to the character of each ingredient.

Made without charcoal or phthalates, Maroma incense brings together traditional craftsmanship and a thoughtful approach to everyday living. Whether used for meditation, a quiet evening or simply to create a welcoming atmosphere, each stick carries a little of the care with which it was made.

Small Choices, Lasting Impact

World Environmental Health Day, 26 September

Caring for the environment is not a single-day commitment. It is something we work towards through the decisions we make every day.

This World Environmental Health Day, we reflected on the practical steps that shape our work in Auroville. From using solar energy to reducing plastic packaging and sorting materials for recycling, we continue to look for ways to reduce our environmental footprint.

Even the smallest remnants can find a new purpose. Leftover candle wax from production is collected and reused, sometimes becoming special candles and sometimes made available to the Auroville community at reduced prices.

These practices reflect a simple belief: responsible production begins with paying attention to what we use, what we leave behind and what can be given another life. There is always more to improve, and every thoughtful choice contributes to that process.

A Little More Love for Your Heart

World Heart Day, 29 September

Good food is one of life's everyday pleasures, and taking care of ourselves can begin with something as simple as a nourishing meal.

For World Heart Day, Maroma Cafe celebrated the connection between mindful eating and personal well-being. Located on Aspiration Street in Auroville, our vegan kitchen brings together carefully selected ingredients, fresh flavours and the pleasure of food prepared with attention.

We believe that eating well should never feel like a compromise. A satisfying meal can be colourful, comforting and full of flavour, while encouraging us to make thoughtful choices about what we eat.

Mindful dining is also about taking time: slowing down, appreciating the ingredients and enjoying the experience of sharing a meal. Sometimes, caring for ourselves begins with being a little more present at the table.

A World of Imagination: The Magic Carpet

4 to 17 October, Aspiration Street, Auroville

Art, imagination and playful experimentation come together in The Magic Carpet, a creative exhibition celebrating the possibilities of making things by hand.

Presented by Gallery Maji and the Women Builder Collective, this Tiny Artist Project invites visitors to explore creativity through materials, textures and unexpected forms. Curated by Supriya Menon Meneghetti, the exhibition shares a space with Maroma and Maji on Aspiration Street.

Alongside the exhibition, hands-on workshops invite participants to experience the pleasure of creating. Waste-fabric art sessions explore new possibilities for discarded textiles, while Dorodango workshops introduce the Japanese art of transforming earth into beautifully polished spheres.

The exhibition runs from 4 to 17 October, daily from 9am to 5pm. The opening was at 4.30pm on 3 October. Waste-fabric workshops take place on 11 and 18 October, Sundays from 10am to 12pm. Dorodango workshops are on 10 and 17 October, Saturdays from 10am to 1pm.

Workshop enquiries: tinyartistproject@gmail.com or womenbuildercollective@gmail.com.
